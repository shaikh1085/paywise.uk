How Much Stamp Duty Will I Pay in the UK? (SDLT 2026/2027)

Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is a tiered property purchase tax payable to HMRC on homes bought in England and Northern Ireland. The rate you pay depends directly on the property purchase price and whether you are a first-time buyer, moving home, or purchasing an additional property.

First-Time Buyers

0% on the first £300,000, then 5% between £300,001 and £500,000 on homes up to £500,000.

Home Movers

0% on the first £125,000, 2% up to £250,000, 5% up to £925,000, 10% up to £1.5m, and 12% above £1.5m.

Second Homes & Buy-to-Let

+5% surcharge on the full price, on top of standard rates (so 5% up to £125k, 7% up to £250k, 10% up to £925k, 15% up to £1.5m, 17% above).

How Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is Calculated in England & NI

Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is a progressive tiered tax paid when purchasing residential property or land in England and Northern Ireland. The tax is charged only on the portion of the property price falling within each specific rate band.

First-time buyers benefit from relief on properties up to £500,000, paying 0% on the first £300,000 and 5% on the portion between £300,001 and £500,000. If the purchase price exceeds £500,000, no first-time buyer relief is available and standard residential rates apply to the entire price.

Purchasers of additional residential properties (including second homes, holiday cottages, and buy-to-let investments) pay a 5% surcharge across every single price band on top of standard residential SDLT rates following the Autumn Budget.

Calculation Assumptions & Rules

  • Calculations apply strictly to residential freehold or leasehold properties in England and Northern Ireland.
  • Scotland applies Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT) and Wales applies Land Transaction Tax (LTT), which have separate devolved schedules.
  • First-time buyer relief assumes neither buyer has previously owned residential property anywhere in the world.
  • Additional property surcharge reflects the 5% surcharge rate introduced in the Autumn Budget.
  • Does not calculate mixed-use, commercial, or complex non-residential land transactions.
