How Much is the Minimum Payment on a Credit Card in the UK?

In the UK, your minimum credit card repayment is the lowest amount you can pay each month to keep your account in good standing and avoid default charges. This calculator assumes interest = balance x APR / 12 and the minimum payment is the greatest of £5, 2.5% of the balance, or 1% of the balance plus monthly interest. Your card issuer's statement may differ slightly. The FCA requires lenders to set a minimum payment in general terms, but individual lenders choose their own formula, so the exact rule varies by card.

1. Percentage Rule

2.5% of your total outstanding statement balance.

2. Interest + 1% Rule

1% of principal balance plus that month's interest (interest = balance x APR / 12).

3. Flat Floor Minimum

A flat minimum floor of £5.00 (or the full balance plus interest, if less).

Why You Should Avoid Paying Only the Minimum Repayment

As your outstanding balance gradually reduces, your credit card issuer recalculates and lowers your required minimum monthly payment. Because your monthly payments shrink along with your debt, the amount going toward paying off the actual principal slows down to a crawl. Setting a standing order for a fixed monthly amount (or moving debt to a 0% balance transfer card) keeps your repayment constant, cutting years off your debt timeline and saving thousands of pounds in interest charges.

How Credit Card Minimum Payments & Interest Work in the UK

UK credit card interest is calculated daily and compounded monthly based on your Annual Percentage Rate (APR). If you carry a balance from month to month, interest is added to your account.

This calculator assumes interest = balance x APR / 12 and the minimum payment is the greatest of £5, 2.5% of the balance, or 1% of the balance plus monthly interest. Your card issuer statement may differ slightly. The FCA requires lenders to set a minimum payment in general terms, but individual lenders choose their own formula, so the exact rule varies by card. Paying only the minimum results in long repayment timelines and excessive interest costs.

Switching to a fixed monthly repayment or paying off your balance within a target timeframe rapidly decreases principal debt and drastically reduces the total interest paid.
