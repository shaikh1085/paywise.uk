How Council Tax Bands Work (A to H)

In England and Scotland, every domestic property is assigned a Council Tax band from Band A (cheapest) to Band H (most expensive) based on its estimated open-market value on 1 April 1991. In Wales, bands A to I are based on a 1 April 2003 valuation list.

Band D as the Benchmark

Councils set their core tax rate for Band D (ratio 9/9 or 100%). All other bands are calculated as fixed statutory fractions of this Band D rate.

Lower Bands (A to C)

Band A pays 6/9 (66.7%), Band B pays 7/9 (77.8%), and Band C pays 8/9 (88.9%) of the local Band D figure.

Higher Bands (E to H)

Band E pays 11/9 (122.2%), Band F pays 13/9 (144.4%), Band G pays 15/9 (166.7%), and Band H pays 18/9 (200.0%) in England/Wales.

Single Person Discount and Other Reductions

UK council tax rules provide statutory discounts for eligible households:

1. Single Person Discount (25% Off):

If you are the only adult (aged 18 or older) living in the property, you receive an automatic 25% discount on your full council tax bill.

2. Disregarded Occupants (Students, Apprentices & Carers):

Full-time university students, live-in care workers, and people with severe mental impairments are "disregarded" for council tax purposes. If everyone in the house is a full-time student, the property is 100% exempt from council tax.

3. Disabled Band Reduction Scheme:

If you or someone living in your home has a substantial disability requiring an extra room, adapted bathroom, or wheelchair use indoors, you can qualify to have your bill charged at the band below your property's actual band.

Why Council Tax Varies by Local Authority

Your final council tax bill is composed of several independent statutory elements:

• Billing Authority Core Charge: Set by your district, borough, or unitary council to fund local services like waste collection, parks, and planning.

• Adult Social Care Precept: An additional dedicated percentage added by county and unitary councils to cover the rising costs of adult social care services.

• Police & Crime Commissioner Precept: Funds local police forces and community safety.

• Fire and Rescue Authority & Parish Councils: Local parish, town council, or fire authority charges.

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to common questions about UK council tax.

In England and Scotland, council tax bands (A to H) are based on what the open-market capital value of the property would have been on 1 April 1991. In Wales, properties were revalued in 2003 (bands A to I). Even newly built homes are assigned a band based on their estimated 1991 (or 2003) equivalent value by the Valuation Office Agency (VOA) or Scottish Assessors.

If you live alone as the sole adult (aged 18 or over) in a property, you are legally entitled to a 25% Single Person Discount off your total council tax bill, regardless of your income or council tax band. Certain individuals are also "disregarded" (not counted as adults), including full-time students, live-in carers, and individuals with severe mental impairments.

Each local authority (billing authority) sets its own annual budget to fund local public services such as social care, bin collections, road maintenance, and libraries. In addition, bills include statutory "precepts" for police and crime commissioners, fire and rescue services, county councils, and parish councils, causing substantial variation in Band D rates across different regions.

You can check your official property council tax band for free online via the GOV.UK Council Tax Valuation service for England and Wales (gov.uk/council-tax-bands) or the Scottish Assessors Association (saa.gov.uk) for properties in Scotland by simply entering your postcode and street address.

By default, local authorities in the UK issue an annual bill split into 10 equal monthly installments from April through January, with two payment-free months in February and March. However, you have a statutory legal right to request your council spread your payments across all 12 calendar months to lower your monthly outflow.

Band D is the national benchmark (ratio 9/9 or 100%). Other bands in England & Wales pay fixed statutory proportions of Band D: Band A is 6/9 (66.7%), Band B is 7/9 (77.8%), Band C is 8/9 (88.9%), Band E is 11/9 (122.2%), Band F is 13/9 (144.4%), Band G is 15/9 (166.7%), and Band H is 18/9 (200.0%). Scotland applies a slightly higher weighting to bands E through H.
