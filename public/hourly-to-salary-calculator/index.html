How Hourly Rates Convert to Annual UK Salary

In the UK, converting an hourly wage into an annual salary is calculated by multiplying your hourly rate by your weekly contracted hours, multiplied by 52 weeks in a calendar year.

Standard Full-Time Hours

Standard full-time contracts in the UK range between 37.5 and 40 hours per week (1,950 to 2,080 hours per year).

Overtime Multipliers

Working overtime hours at time-and-a-half (1.5x) or double-time (2.0x) increases gross income and is taxed at your current marginal rate.

Holiday Pay Rights

All UK workers have a statutory right to 5.6 weeks of paid holiday per year (equivalent to 12.07% of hours worked for irregular workers).

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to common questions about UK hourly rates and take-home pay.

Multiply your hourly rate by the number of contracted hours per week, then multiply by 52 (the number of weeks in a year). For example, £18/hour x 37.5 hours/week x 52 weeks = £35,100 gross annual salary.

The National Living Wage applies to workers aged 21 and over (currently £11.44 per hour). Separate lower statutory rates apply to 18–20 year olds, under 18s, and apprentices.

Overtime hours paid at enhanced multipliers (e.g. 1.5x time-and-a-half or 2.0x double time) increase your gross earnings. However, extra income is taxed at your current marginal rate (20% or 40% income tax plus 8% or 2% NI).

Yes. Almost all UK workers are legally entitled to 5.6 weeks of paid annual leave per year (28 days for someone working 5 days a week, pro-rata for part-time workers).

National Insurance is calculated based on your specific earnings period (each week if paid weekly, or each calendar month if paid monthly). Unlike Income Tax, Class 1 NI is non-cumulative.

Under UK holiday pay guidance, irregular-hours and zero-hours workers accrue statutory holiday entitlement at the rate of 12.07% of the actual hours worked in each pay period.
