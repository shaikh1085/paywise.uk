How Contractor Day Rates Compare to Permanent UK Salaries

When transitioning from permanent employment to contracting in the UK, daily contract rates appear substantially higher at first glance. However, contractors do not receive statutory benefits such as paid annual leave, paid bank holidays, sick pay, or employer pension contributions.

Billable Days in a Year

A standard working year has 260 weekdays (52 weeks x 5 days). After deducting 8 UK bank holidays and 25–30 days of annual leave and bench time, full-time contractors typically bill 220 to 230 days per year.

Business Expenses & Overheads

Operating as a contractor involves costs for professional indemnity insurance, accountancy fees, software licenses, equipment, and self-funded training.

Pensions & Sick Leave

Without employer auto-enrolment contributions (minimum 3%), contractors must self-fund private pension contributions and emergency cash buffers for unpaid sick leave.

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to common questions about UK day rates and take-home pay.

Multiply your daily rate by your expected billable days (typically 220 to 230 days after subtracting 25–33 days of holidays, bank holidays, training, and bench time). Then deduct business insurance, pension contributions, and accountancy fees to find the equivalent permanent salary.

A standard calendar year contains 260 weekdays (52 weeks x 5 days). After deducting 8 UK bank holidays and 25 days of annual leave/sick/bench allowance, full-time contractors typically bill between 220 and 230 days per year.

Outside IR35 contractors trading via a limited company can deduct legitimate business expenses like professional indemnity insurance, travel, home office costs, equipment, and accountancy fees before corporation tax.

Outside IR35 contracts allow you to operate via your own limited company, taking salary and dividends with greater tax efficiency. Inside IR35 contracts treat you as an employee for tax purposes, subjecting income to PAYE and employer NI.

Independent contractors do not receive paid holiday or sick leave unless employed via an umbrella company that has factored statutory holiday pay (12.07%) into the contract rate agreement.

Limited company contractors should set aside 19% to 25% of net company profits for Corporation Tax, and maintain separate funds for quarterly VAT liabilities (standard 20% or Flat Rate Scheme).
