UK Student Loan Repayment Plans Explained

In the UK, student loans function as an income-contingent graduate tax deducted through PAYE. You only repay if your gross earnings exceed your plan’s statutory threshold.

Plan 1

Threshold: £26,900 / year. 9% deducted above threshold. Applies to courses starting 1998–2011 (England/Wales) or Northern Ireland.

Plan 2

Threshold: £29,385 / year. 9% deducted above threshold. Applies to undergraduate courses between Sept 2012 and July 2023.

Plan 4 (Scotland)

Threshold: £33,795 / year. 9% deducted above threshold. Applies to Scottish students funded via SAAS.

Plan 5

Threshold: £25,000 / year. 9% deducted above threshold. Applies to new courses starting from 1 August 2023.

Postgraduate Loan

Threshold: £21,000 / year. 6% deducted above threshold. Deducted concurrently alongside undergraduate loans.

If your gross earnings drop below your plan’s statutory threshold in any given pay period, student loan deductions automatically stop until your earnings rise above the threshold again.

Repayments are 9% of gross earnings above your plan's threshold for undergraduate loans (Plan 1 £26,900, Plan 2 £29,385, Plan 4 £33,795, Plan 5 £25,000) and 6% above £21,000 for postgraduate loans, deducted through PAYE. For example, a Plan 2 graduate earning £35,000 pays 9% of £5,615, which is £505.35 a year, about £42.11 a month. If you have both an undergraduate and a postgraduate loan, both deductions run at the same time. Enter your salary and plan above for an exact figure.

Unlike credit cards, you do not choose the order through PAYE: if you have more than one plan, each deduction is taken at the same time on earnings above its own threshold. Voluntary overpayments are a separate decision and are not usually the best use of money for Plan 2 and Plan 5 loans, because unpaid balances are written off after 30 or 40 years and repayments stop if earnings fall below the threshold. This calculator shows your mandatory monthly repayment for each plan; it does not rank loans for voluntary overpayment, so take regulated advice before paying extra.
